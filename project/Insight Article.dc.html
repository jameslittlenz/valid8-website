Putting the DISTF Reference Architecture to work

What the nine chapters cover, and where to start if you are building a digital identity service in Aotearoa.

James LittleNew Zealand Lead · 2 October 2026
in ⧉

The Digital Identity Services Trust Framework Act 2023 sets the rules for digital identity services in New Zealand. The Reference Architecture is the practical blueprint for putting those rules into operation.

It is publicly available, and it is the common reference point for everyone building digital identity services in Aotearoa. If you are planning to issue credentials, build a wallet, or accept digital identity in your service, this is where to begin.

What it defines

Across nine chapters, annexes and supporting implementation guidance, the architecture sets out what is needed to issue, hold and present digital identity credentials securely, with accessibility, inclusion and privacy built in.

RolesWho issues, holds and relies on a credential
Trust modelHow each party knows it can rely on the others
Assurance levelsHow much confidence a credential carries
Data modelWhat a credential contains and how it is structured
StandardsThe protocols and formats every service uses

Built with the sector

Valid8 Advisory led the co-design, drafting and consultation, commissioned by the Government Digital Delivery Agency (then the Department of Internal Affairs). Over 40 organisations across the public and private sectors took part through regular workshops in person and online, whiteboard sessions, draft review and consultation, and collective contribution and endorsement.

The common reference point for everyone building digital identity services in Aotearoa New Zealand.

Aligned internationally

The architecture aligns with international standards and practice to support cross-border interoperability, so a credential issued in New Zealand can work alongside those issued elsewhere.

ISO/IEC 18013-5 ISO/IEC 23220 W3C IETF OpenID EU

Where to start

  1. 1Identify your role. Work out whether you will issue, hold or rely on credentials, and read the chapters that apply.
  2. 2Assess your readiness. Compare your current systems and processes against the requirements.
  3. 3Plan your path to accreditation. Decide which services to accredit and how you will meet the identification management, privacy and security requirements.
